## CRITICAL findings (named strategy/formula/major concept entirely absent)

1. **CHADTP (Conners-Hayward Advance-Decline Trading Patterns)** — Ch.12. A specific smoothed-breadth reversal system (5-day advance/decline sum, ±400 or modernized SD-based extreme thresholds, combined with an S&P point-penetration trigger). The general "breadth as countertrend" theme is well covered via the separate **Connors AD-Ratio System** (which IS in `03_mean_reversion.md`), but CHADTP itself — a distinct, more elaborate named system with its own construction and entry logic — does not appear anywhere. Target: `03_mean_reversion.md` or `13_indicator_reference.md` (near the existing Connors AD-Ratio entry).

2. **Dogs of the Dow (O'Higgins) + Small Dogs + Foolish Four** — Ch.22. A widely-known named value strategy (buy the 10 highest-yield Dow stocks annually; Small Dogs = lowest-priced of the 10; Foolish Four = Sheard's 4-stock refinement) with a cited backtest (10.8%/yr 1992-2011 vs. S&P 9.6%/yr). Completely absent. Note: the *related but distinct* Ch.13 system — "Dogs of the Dow / Best-Return Dow Rebalance with Volatility Hedge," which inverts the theory (buy best-performers, monthly rebalance, vol-triggered short hedge) — is also absent (searched "Dogs of the Dow," "Foolish Four": zero hits across all 18 files). Target: `03_mean_reversion.md` (Dogs of the Dow, mean-reversion/value logic) and `11_risk_management.md` or `12_portfolio_construction.md` (the volatility-hedge overlay variant).

3. **Fischer's Golden Section Compass (GSC) System** — Ch.14. Two named sub-techniques: **GSC Time-Goal Days** (Fibonacci-derived day-count projections of reversal dates from paired swing extremes, with a 5-day-reversal entry trigger and stop rule) and **GSC Price Goals** (Wave 3/5 price objectives via the 1.618 multiplier, with an explicit Wave-3-must-precede-Wave-5-target invalidation rule). Zero hits for "GSC" or "Golden Section" across the knowledge base. Target: `07_market_regimes.md` (near the existing Elliott Wave / Gann material) or a new cycle/time-projection subsection.

4. **David Aronson's Descriptor/Trade-Profile Pattern Recognition method** — Ch.15. A named methodology (define a "trade profile" of necessary-but-not-sufficient conditions, e.g. ≥3% move within 20 days with no interim loss >0.5%; computer-locate all historical qualifying instances; test candidate indicators for concentration at the labeled points). This is a distinct, citable methodology (Aronson) — not to be confused with Pardo's unrelated "trade profile vs. evaluation profile" live-monitoring concept, which IS covered in `09_exits.md`/`11_risk_management.md`/`18_common_failure_modes.md` and was initially mistaken for this entry during the audit. Zero coverage of Aronson's actual method anywhere. Notably, Kaufman's own `Research_Ideas.md` idea #55 explicitly proposes this as a candidate ML-labeling method for crypto — that research idea also did not make it into `16_research_hypotheses.md` (see MODERATE finding on research-idea underrepresentation below). Target: `14_backtesting_and_validation.md` (pattern-discovery/labeling methodology) or `16_research_hypotheses.md`.

5. **Bill Williams' 5-Bar Fractal Breakout strategy** — Ch.20. The Awesome Oscillator indicator (Bill Williams) IS well covered in `05_momentum.md` and `13_indicator_reference.md`, but the separate, fully-specified **5-Bar Fractal Breakout trading system** (up/down fractal pivot definitions, breakout-of-fractal entry, fractal-extreme stop, profit target = fractal range, backtested 58% reliability on SPY 2000-2018) is entirely absent. Target: `04_breakouts.md`.

6. **Gordon Gustafson's Price Persistency Strategy** — Ch.22. A named long-only Martingale-style mean-reversion system (buy after 4 consecutive lower closes, exit after a 5-day up-run or 8-day time cap) with two cited backtests (78% win rate 1988-1995; 76% win rate extended to 2001) and an explicit author caveat about it being validated only within a single bull-market regime. Zero hits for "Gustafson." This connects directly to the Theory of Runs material (up-run-bias, Table 22.3) which IS covered elsewhere — but the named strategy itself is missing. Target: `03_mean_reversion.md` (alongside the other Martingale/Anti-Martingale entries, which ARE present).

7. **Commodex** — Ch.22. A long-running (since 1959), still-commercially-operated named system: dual-MA crossover ranked into 3 scored components (long-term MA, short-term MA, fast-vs-slow position), combined with open-interest and volume momentum into a weighted trend index, with a distinctive non-standard rule (falling OI + falling price treated as bullish) and tiered profit-taking (50%/100% profit triggers). Zero hits. Target: `02_trend_following.md` (multi-factor composite trend systems section).

8. **Rating-Service Basket Spread** — Ch.13. A generalizable methodology for converting any stock-ranking service (fundamental or self-built technical) into a systematic long/short basket spread, with explicit position-sizing (volatility-adjusted, ≥20% divergence rebalancing rule) and a built-in validation step for whether the ranking is trend-confirming or mean-reverting. Zero hits for "Rating-Service" or "Rating Service." Target: `03_mean_reversion.md` or `12_portfolio_construction.md`.

9. **Butterfly Spread (delivery-month term-structure arbitrage)** and **Trend Following Using Bull and Bear Spreads** — Ch.13. Two named intramarket-spread techniques described in general methodology terms (not fully parameterized in the source, but substantive enough to warrant an entry): the Butterfly Spread exploits fund-roll-driven term-structure distortions (sell 2 middle-month contracts, buy 1 each of the surrounding months); the Bull/Bear Spread substitutes an intramarket calendar spread for an outright trend position in grain/food markets. `Trend of Ratio (Crude/Gold)` from the same chapter IS covered (found in `03_mean_reversion.md`), but these two siblings are not. Target: `03_mean_reversion.md` or a spreads-specific subsection.

10. **Ch.20 "soft AI / expert system" trio: Technical Expert System (Fishman/Barr/Loick), Merloti's Fuzzy Expert System, and the Game-Theoretic Trend-vs-Mean-Reversion Payoff Matrix** — all three zero-hit. These are conceptually important because (a) the Technical Expert System is Kaufman's worked example of probabilistic (confidence-scored) rather than binary system output; (b) the Merloti system carries an explicit, important warning that synthetic-data backtest results cannot validate a real strategy ("you cannot yet rely on the test results from synthetic data") — directly relevant methodology for `14_backtesting_and_validation.md`'s overfitting-risk material; (c) the payoff matrix is a clean illustrative lesson that trend and mean-reversion paradigms "net out to be almost the same" when each uses its own optimal discipline. Target: `14_backtesting_and_validation.md` (Merloti's synthetic-data warning is the highest-value piece to recover) and `18_common_failure_modes.md`.

11. **Hedge Fund Replication (Ch.20 static/dynamic methods)** — distinct from the Ch.8 "Replication" portfolio-copying technique which IS covered. The Ch.20 version (Excel Solver-based static allocation-matching, and a daily-rebalanced dynamic tracking-error-minimizing variant, with the ~85%-replication/15%-tracking-error benchmark) is a separate, more developed treatment and is absent. Target: `12_portfolio_construction.md`.

## Summary

- **CRITICAL**: 11 findings — entirely absent named systems (CHADTP, Dogs of the Dow/Foolish Four, GSC Time-Goal/Price-Goal, Aronson's descriptor method, 5-Bar Fractal Breakout, Gustafson's Persistency Strategy, Commodex, Rating-Service Basket Spread, Butterfly Spread + Bull/Bear Spread, Ch.20's expert-system/payoff-matrix trio, Hedge Fund Replication).

- **MINOR**: 3 findings — low-priority, unconfirmed candidates for a future spare-budget pass.

**Overall completeness assessment**: Very strong. Indicator coverage (`13_indicator_reference.md` plus the momentum/trend/volatility files) is comprehensive at an estimated ~93%. Strategy coverage is comprehensive for the book's methodologically central chapters (trend systems, breakouts, adaptive techniques, risk control, portfolio construction — all >90%) but measurably thinner (~76%) in the mid-book range covering spreads/arbitrage sub-variants, behavioral/sentiment named systems, and short-term-pattern research ideas.

**Chapter range most at risk of under-coverage**: **Ch.9-16**, specifically the intersection of Ch.13 (Spreads and Arbitrage — several named spread/basket sub-systems beyond the well-covered pairs-trading core), Ch.14 (the GSC system), and Ch.15-16 (both the Aronson descriptor method and the wholesale absence of these two chapters' research ideas from `16_research_hypotheses.md`). A secondary risk pocket is Ch.20 (Advanced Techniques) within the Ch.17-24 range, where the "soft AI" expert-system trio and Hedge Fund Replication are missing despite the chapter's otherwise-strong adaptive-technique coverage.
