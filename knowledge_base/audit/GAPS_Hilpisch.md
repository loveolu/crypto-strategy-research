# Gap Audit: Hilpisch, *Python for Algorithmic Trading* vs. knowledge_base/

Audit method: read all 21 files in `Knowledge\Hilpisch_Python_for_Algorithmic_Trading\` (10 chapters, Overview, Appendix, and all 9 topic-extraction files) as ground truth; cross-checked against every `knowledge_base\*.md` file that references "Hilpisch" (15 of 18 files) plus targeted greps for architecture-specific terms (`AdaBoost`, `LRVectorBacktester`, `tpqoa`, `ZeroMQ`, `online algorithm`, etc.) across all 18 files.

## CRITICAL

### C1. The "online algorithm" transformation pattern — entirely absent as a documented concept
- **Source**: Hilpisch Ch.7 ("Signal Generation in Real Time — Online Algorithms"), Ch.8 (`MomentumTrader.on_success`), Ch.10 (`MLTrader.on_success`, persisted-model hand-off). This is named in the book's own `Master_Summary.md` as **Author Insight #8, "the single reusable architecture"** connecting real-time sockets, broker streaming, and ML deployment.
- **What's missing**: the four-step pattern — (1) collect incoming ticks into a growing DataFrame, (2) resample to a fixed bar length, (3) **drop the still-incomplete last/current bar** before computing signals (`iloc[:-1]` or reading `iloc[-2]` under `label='right'` resampling — the concrete anti-look-ahead technique for live bar construction), (4) predict/signal and conditionally place an order. Also missing: the offline→online transformation framing itself (an "offline algorithm" trained on a complete historical dataset vs. an "online algorithm" that only knows current/past state, with no foresight — explicitly the live-trading analogue of the vectorized-vs-event-based look-ahead distinction).
- **Where it should live**: `14_backtesting_and_validation.md` (as a new subsection near §12, e.g. "12.5 The Online Algorithm Pattern: Offline→Online Transformation") and/or a cross-reference in `18_common_failure_modes.md` under look-ahead bias, since the `iloc[-2]`/drop-incomplete-bar technique is a concrete, reusable defense against a specific live-trading look-ahead bug that has no analogue elsewhere in this knowledge base's five source books.
- **Current KB state**: zero coverage. The only trace is a name-drop of `MomentumTrader`/`MLTrader` in `13_indicator_reference.md` line 122 ("live `MomentumTrader`/`MLTrader` real-time momentum feature (5s/10s bars)") with no explanation of the architecture, the incomplete-bar problem, or the pattern's generality.

- The **hit-ratio metric definition** (`sign(actual_return * predicted_return)`) and the concrete progression it's used to illustrate: regressing on raw lagged returns gives hit ratio ≈49.9% (coin-flip); regressing on lagged returns against `sign(return)` as the target improves it to ≈51.9%.

## MODERATE

### M1. Hilpisch's own Kelly-criterion derivation is not cross-referenced in the KB's dedicated Kelly section
- **Source**: Risk_Management.md / Ch.10 — the binomial derivation (`f* = p - q` for a simple 50/50-stakes game, e.g. p=0.55 → f*=0.10) and the continuous/stock-market generalization (`G∞(f) = r + (μ-r)·f - (σ²/2)·f²`, optimized at **f\* = (μ-r)/σ²**, explicitly compared to and distinguished from the Sharpe ratio: "looks similar... but is different" — Sharpe divides by volatility σ, Kelly divides by variance σ²). The worked S&P 500 example (μ≈9.99%, σ≈14.76%, r=0 → f\*≈4.59, i.e. 459% leverage) and the simulation showing higher-than-Kelly fractions increase volatility without reliably increasing average ending capital.
- **Current KB state**: `10_position_sizing.md` §2 is titled "Kelly Criterion — Three Treatments" and covers Vince, Chan, and Kaufman only — Hilpisch is absent from this section entirely, despite his `f*=(μ-r)/σ²` formula being structurally identical to Chan's `f=m/s²` (an independent-convergence data point the file already makes for Vince/Chan/Kaufman elsewhere but misses for Hilpisch). Hilpisch's concrete numeric worked example (S&P 500, f*≈4.59, half-Kelly≈2.3) and his own explicit Kelly-vs-Sharpe distinction are absent everywhere in the KB — a search for "4.59" or "half Kelly" applied to Hilpisch's own instrument-level example returns nothing outside the raw extraction files.
- **Where it should live**: `10_position_sizing.md` §2, as a fourth treatment alongside Vince/Chan/Kaufman (the section's own synthesis paragraph — "all three treat the raw Kelly output as a ceiling... should be diluted in practice" — should become "all four").

## Summary

**Total findings**: 4 CRITICAL, 4 MODERATE (one explicitly a non-finding/scope confirmation), 2 MINOR.

**Overall completeness assessment**: The KB's coverage of Hilpisch is **strong on backtesting methodology and the three "classical" strategies (SMA, momentum, mean-reversion)** — `14_backtesting_and_validation.md` §12 in particular is a faithful, detailed, well-cited reproduction of the vectorized-vs-event-based architecture distinction, the data-snooping/overfitting self-critique, and the transaction-cost-reversal findings, and the three classical strategies are fully represented with worked numbers in their respective topic files. However, the KB is **meaningfully thinner than the source on everything downstream of Ch.5**: the four ML-based strategy families (linear regression, logistic regression, DNN, AdaBoost) are named but not architecturally documented; the online-algorithm live-deployment pattern, broker API integration mechanics, and Ch.10's cloud/logging/monitoring material are essentially absent. Given the source book's own structure — roughly the first half (Ch.1–6) is backtesting/strategy, the second half (Ch.7–10) is real-time/live-deployment — the KB has captured close to 90% of the fidelity of the first half and closer to 15–20% of the second half. Weighted across the whole book, a reasonable overall estimate is **~55–60% content fidelity**, concentrated unevenly: excellent on methodology/overfitting/classical strategies, weak on ML-strategy mechanics and live-deployment architecture.

**Do all 7 strategy families appear in the KB?**
1. SMA crossover — YES, `02_trend_following.md`, fully covered.
2. Time-series momentum — YES, `05_momentum.md`, fully covered.
3. Mean-reversion (SMA-distance) — YES, `03_mean_reversion.md`, fully covered.
4. Linear-regression direction prediction — **NAME ONLY**, one sentence in `08_entries.md`; no lag-construction, hit-ratio math, or class architecture anywhere.
5. Logistic-regression classification — **NAME ONLY**, same sentence as #4; no scikit-learn API pattern, no GLD hit-ratio numbers, no class architecture.
6. DNN classification — **NAME ONLY**, one sentence plus a hit-ratio-disconnect mention; no architecture, reproducibility technique, or feature-engineering detail.
7. AdaBoost live-deployed strategy — **NAME ONLY**, one sentence; the book's own most complete "idea to cloud" pipeline (backtest → risk analysis → Kelly leverage → persistence → live deployment) is not walked through anywhere in the KB.

**Single most important gap**: The AdaBoost strategy (family #7) is the book's central capstone narrative — the only strategy in the entire five-book knowledge base that is taken all the way from backtest through risk analysis through Kelly-sized leverage through model persistence through live cloud deployment with real-time monitoring — and in the consolidated KB it survives as one sentence. Everything that makes it instructive (the online-algorithm transformation, the broker-API live-trading class, the VaR/drawdown risk analysis at 30x leverage, the ZeroMQ monitoring architecture) is either scattered as disconnected fragments or missing entirely.
